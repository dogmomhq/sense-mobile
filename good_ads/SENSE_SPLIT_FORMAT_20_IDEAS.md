# SENSE — 20 Split-Screen Concepts (Locked Format v2)

## The locked format
**TOP:** live reaction cam — gen-Z gamer energy, streamer-room aesthetic (RGB glow, ring light, mic in frame, hood up / claw clips / gamer girl fits), shot like a phone propped against a monitor. Hot girls as the recurring reactors, with a rotating cast so it feels like a community, not one actress.
**BOTTOM:** hyper-organic home-shot animal clip (shaky phone, bad framing at first — reads 100% real) → the real Sense UI slides in → tap, clock stops → smash-cut win with the reaction-cam energy peaking → new exit screen (avatars, YOUR OPPONENT IS WAITING, pulsing PLAY NOW).

## Why this format is the one (what the ad data + algorithm actually reward)
TikTok's own numbers say creator-style authentic UGC drives ~70% higher CTR than polished brand ads, and 2026 gaming-ad meta is micro-story hooks in the first 3 seconds — drama, fails, relatable frustration — not gameplay mechanics ([MegaDigital 2026 trends](https://megadigital.ai/en/blog/tiktok-ads-trends-for-mobile-games/), [Udonis guide](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/mobile-game-ads)). Our format is engineered for exactly that: the bottom half IS organic viral content (animal videos are the FYP's native species), the top half is a face feeling things (the most-watched content category), and the win sync is a built-in rewatch loop. The algorithm can't tell it's an ad until second 4 — that's the whole trick.

Algorithm rules baked into every concept below: hook readable with sound OFF in 1.5s · her face reacts BEFORE the reveal (forces a rewatch to see what she saw) · one comment-bait element per ad · loop the last frame into the first where possible.

## The 20

**1. "CHAT IS THIS REAL?"** Top: streamer girl mid-broadcast leans into the lens, whispers "chat… is this real?" Bottom: raccoon opening the fridge like it pays rent. UI in, she taps, wins, headset flies off. *Why:* "chat" framing = native streamer language; the whisper-to-scream arc is the highest-contrast reaction curve you can build in 8 seconds.

**2. The Duo Squeeze.** Top: two girls sharing one gaming chair, fighting over who taps. Bottom: moose in the garage. They tap simultaneously, win, double scream, chair tips backward out of frame. *Why:* two faces double the reaction surface; the chair fall is a physical punchline that loops.

**3. Nail Tap ASMR.** Top: extreme close-up of long acrylic nails hovering over the screen, ring light glinting, dead silence — then one decisive tap. Cut up to her smirk. Bottom: cougar on the kitchen counter. *Why:* the pre-tap silence is a pattern interrupt in a loud feed; nail-tap ASMR is its own massive niche and it makes the TIMER the star.

**4. Rage Quit Girl (Loss Ad).** Top: she's on an 8-streak, cocky, blowing a bubble with her gum. Bottom: baby elephant in a kiddie pool. WHO WAS FASTER: opponent by 0.06s. Bubble pops. Controller-throw energy, headphones off, dead stare into lens. *Why:* losses out-perform wins for comments and credibility; the 0.06s margin makes every viewer think "I'm faster than her."

**5. The 3AM Queue.** Top: dark room, face lit only by the phone, blanket hood, whispering so she doesn't wake anyone — silent fist-pump win with a bitten-lip scream. Bottom: owl staring through the bedroom window (it's watching her play). *Why:* 3AM-gaming is core gen-Z relatable lore; the owl watching HER is a top-bottom crossover gag people rewatch to catch.

**6. Boyfriend Bet.** Top: she plays while her boyfriend leans in behind her chair talking trash — she wins HIS $100, he walks out of frame, she spins the chair. Bottom: bison in the backyard pool. *Why:* couple-competition is evergreen viral; his walk-off is the shareable GIF.

**7. The Recruiter.** Top: she can't decide, panics, physically drags her little sister into frame and points at the screen; sister taps instantly, deadpan, walks away; big sister screams at the win alone. Bottom: near-identical caiman vs alligator round. *Why:* phone-handoff seeds real user behavior, and "the casual is better than the sweat" is a beloved gaming trope.

**8. Mukbang Interrupted.** Top: girl mid-mukbang (chips, mic close), challenge notification interrupts, she answers with clean fingers' knuckle-tap, wins, chip crumbs everywhere on the scream. Bottom: goat that walked into a McDonald's. *Why:* format-collision (mukbang × gaming) reads as organic FYP chaos, not an ad — the algorithm's favorite disguise.

**9. Gym Girl Queue.** Top: mid-set on a leg press, phone buzzes — answers between reps, racks the weight ON the win scream, plates slam as the impact sound. Bottom: kangaroo in a garage gym stealing a resistance band. *Why:* GymTok crossover audience; the plate-slam synced to the win burst is a physical bass drop.

**10. The Translator.** Top: she reacts in rapid-fire Spanish (subtitled), universal scream at the end needs no translation. Bottom: chupacabra-looking hairless dog in a laundry room — answer is XOLO. *Why:* multilingual UGC massively widens the audience pool cheaply, and the exotic-answer round is elite comment bait.

**11. Sibling Sabotage.** Top: little brother keeps reaching over her shoulder to tap wrong; she blocks him with an elbow, taps right, wins, gloats directly into his face. Bottom: peacock that took over the trampoline. *Why:* sibling chaos is a permanent viral genre; the elbow-block is the loopable micro-moment.

**12. The Unbothered Queen.** Top: full skincare mask, cucumber slices ON her eyes — lifts ONE cucumber, glances, taps, replaces cucumber. Win screen erupts; she doesn't move. Caption: "she's him." Bottom: capybara in a hot tub (matching spa energy). *Why:* top-bottom aesthetic mirroring (spa girl / spa capybara) is a galaxy-brain detail comment sections live for; the non-reaction IS the reaction.

**13. Reaction Chain.** Top: three stacked mini-cams (her, her mom, her grandma) all reacting to the same round — grandma answers first. Bottom: wolf on the porch caught on doorbell cam. *Why:* three generations = maximum shareability ("tag your mom"); grandma winning is the anti-expectation punchline that carries the format.

**14. The Fake-Out Scroll.** Top starts FULL SCREEN as just the animal clip (pure organic), then the screen "gets grabbed" and shrinks to the bottom half as her reaction cam slides in on top mid-gasp: "WAIT. this is a question?!" *Why:* the format reveal itself is the hook — it weaponizes the organic-content disguise and teaches the game in one motion.

**15. Duet Bait.** Top half is EMPTY with "duet this — react to your opponent" text; bottom runs the round solo. We post our girl's version as the first duet. *Why:* native TikTok duet mechanics turn the ad into a template; every duet is free distribution with a built-in reaction — the format farming itself.

**16. The Delivery Interruption.** Top: she's answering the door for a food delivery, phone buzzes, she answers the round one-handed while taking the bag, driver visibly watches the win screen and mouths "what app is that?" Bottom: deer in the living room (callback to the proven ad). *Why:* the stranger's curiosity models the viewer's exact question; callbacks build franchise memory.

**17. Heartbeat Round.** Top: her heartbeat audio thumps faster as the clock runs; screen glow pulses red on her face; tap — silence — then the win explosion. Bottom: mountain lion at the sliding glass door, inches from the camera. *Why:* borrowed horror-game grammar (2026's top-performing hook genre per the trend data) applied to a trivia timer — tension the format has never had.

**18. The Apology Video.** Top: she starts in fake-crying "influencer apology" pose — "I need to be honest with you guys…" beat "…I've won $held-up-fingers today." Bottom: goose security guard escorting a mailman. *Why:* parodying TikTok's most recognizable video genre = instant cultural fluency; the fake-out is a 10/10 first-3-seconds hook.

**19. Rank-Up Montage.** Top: rapid jump-cuts of the same girl reacting across five wins — hair progressively messier, room progressively darker, energy drink cans stacking — final win in full gremlin mode. Bottom: five-round animal montage cut to the beat. *Why:* progression montage is gaming-culture native (rank grind), shows repeat play, and the gremlin-decay gag rewards a full watch.

**20. The Callout.** Top: she looks dead into the camera: "I'm 23-0 today. Duet me if you think you're faster." Screen-taps toward the lens like she's challenging YOU. Bottom: hardest round in the bank (nutria vs beaver vs muskrat vs capybara). *Why:* direct-address challenge converts viewers from spectators to opponents — the exact identity shift that installs the app — and pairs with #15 as a self-feeding duet economy.

## Production notes
- Reactor cast: 3-4 locked AI faces (our braided girl + 2-3 new gen-Z gamer girls, face-locked in the forge pipeline) so the "community" is reusable across all 20.
- Every bottom clip: shot "badly" on purpose for the first 1.5s — tilt, refocus, a thumb edge — then stabilizes as the UI lands. The jank IS the credibility.
- Win sync remains law: top reaction peak = bottom win burst = tap SFX + cheer + impact on the same frame.
- Batch economics: 1 top reaction clip pairs with 3-4 different bottom animals — 6 reaction shoots yields 20+ finished ads.
